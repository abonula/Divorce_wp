Understanding the Process

What Is Collaborative Divorce?

A voluntary, structured process that keeps you in control of your divorce — and out of the courtroom.

The Basics

A different kind of divorce

Collaborative divorce is a legal process that allows couples to resolve all divorce-related issues — property division, parenting plans, financial support — through a series of structured, voluntary meetings with a team of trained professionals.

Unlike traditional contested divorce, there are no courtroom battles, no depositions, and no judge deciding the outcome of your family's future. You and your spouse remain in control of every decision.

"Both parties and their attorneys agree, in writing, not to litigate. This commitment fundamentally changes the entire negotiation dynamic — from adversarial to collaborative."

If the collaborative process breaks down and either party chooses to go to court, both collaborative attorneys must withdraw. This structure keeps everyone focused on resolution and gives the process meaningful integrity.

The Full Process

Step by step

Most collaborative divorces move through these stages over six to twelve months.

1

Separate Consultations

Each spouse meets privately with a collaborative attorney to discuss the process, understand their rights, and sign a participation agreement committing to resolve issues out of court.

2

Assemble the Team

Both attorneys help select neutral professionals: a financial neutral to analyze assets and support, and a coach or child specialist if needed for your family's situation.

3

Information Gathering

All financial documents, property records, and relevant information are shared openly — no subpoenas, no discovery battles, no hidden assets.

4

Joint Sessions

The full team meets in facilitated sessions to work through each issue — property division, parenting, support — at a pace that works for your family.

5

Reaching Agreement

Once all issues are resolved, your attorneys draft a comprehensive settlement agreement that reflects everything decided together in your sessions.

6

Court Filing

Your attorneys submit the agreement to the court for approval. In most cases, neither party appears before a judge — it's an administrative step.

7

Moving Forward

With a fair agreement in place and your relationships intact, your family can begin the next chapter with clarity, mutual respect, and confidence.

8

Ongoing Support

CDGW professionals remain a resource for future modifications — parenting plan updates, support changes, or other adjustments your family may need.

Is It Right for You?

When collaborative divorce works well

It isn't right for every situation, but collaborative divorce tends to work best when both parties share some of the following:

Children Involved

Parents who need a lasting co-parenting relationship benefit greatly from the cooperative, non-adversarial approach that collaborative divorce offers.

Complex Finances

Businesses, retirement accounts, real estate, and investments are best handled by a neutral financial expert guiding the full conversation.

Desire for Privacy

Collaborative proceedings are confidential. Nothing goes on public record unless both parties agree to the final settlement terms.
