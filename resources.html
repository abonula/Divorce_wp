FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Detailed answers to the most common questions about collaborative divorce.

Yes. Michigan passed the Collaborative Law Act, which provides a legal framework for collaborative proceedings across the state. CDGW members serve the Southeast Michigan region, but trained collaborative professionals practice statewide.
Browse our member directory and contact a collaborative attorney in your area. They'll schedule an initial consultation to explain the process and determine whether it's appropriate for your situation. There is no obligation after that first meeting.
Either party can withdraw from the collaborative process at any time. If that happens, both collaborative attorneys must withdraw, and new attorneys must be retained for any subsequent litigation. The collaborative attorneys are not permitted to represent either party in court.
You don't need to be perfectly amicable — many couples begin the collaborative process feeling hurt, angry, or uncertain. What matters is that both parties are willing to engage in good faith and commit to resolving issues without court. The mental health professionals on your team are there specifically to help manage difficult emotions during the process.
Yes. Business valuation is handled by the neutral financial professional on your team, who may bring in a specialized business appraiser if needed. Because both parties share one neutral financial expert rather than each hiring their own dueling experts, the process is typically less adversarial and more cost-efficient than litigation.
